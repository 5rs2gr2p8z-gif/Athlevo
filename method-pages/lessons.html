The Athlevo Method

Running coaching lessons for working adults who train in the heat.

The Athlevo Method is a way of training runners who have a job, a family, poor sleep some weeks and hot, humid mornings. It does not invent new physiology. It applies established endurance science to that group of runners, and it counts the full cost of training: the runs, the weather, your work, your sleep and the state of your legs.

These lessons explain how it works, and why each decision in an Athlevo plan is made. They are written for runners who want to understand their training, and for coaches who want to see how a system like this fits together.

What is research and what is our own design

The zone names, session names, weekly structure and decision rules in these lessons are Athlevo's own design. The ideas they draw on come from published work, and each lesson links the sources it relies on. Where a lesson states a research finding, it says so. Nothing here is medical advice.

Questions

What is the Athlevo Method?

It is a total-load endurance training framework. It uses four training zones, a weekly structure built around one main goal, and a set of decision rules that account for heat, work stress and sleep as well as running.

Who is it for?

It is built for working adults who run in hot, humid, real-world conditions, starting with runners in the Philippines and places with similar demands. Beginners, returning runners and experienced racers all start from where they are.

Who wrote it?

Dean Castro, founder and head coach at Athlevo. The lessons come from the Athlevo Method course, version 1.0.

Do I need a lactate meter?

No. Zones are set from effort, pace and heart rate. The lactate numbers in the lessons are reference points for coaches. The training zones lesson explains how they line up.

Where can I get a plan built on this method?

Athlevo coaching programs are built on it. You can see them on the Athlevo programs page.
