Why race pace comes last: the pace ladder

By Dean Castro, Founder and Head Coach at Athlevo. Updated 14 November 2026. 2 minute read.

Race pace is a destination, not a starting point. The pace ladder is the idea that you build the paces above and below race pace first, so goal pace feels sustainable when you reach it.

In short

  • Many runners build an easy base and then jump straight to race-pace intervals.
  • Funnel periodization starts general and narrows toward race pace.
  • Earlier qualities are kept, with small doses added, not dropped.
  • Athlevo keeps both faster and slower paces in the plan for the whole block.

On this page

The gap in the middle of the ladder

A common pattern is to build an easy aerobic base and then jump to race-pace intervals. What gets skipped is the middle of the pace ladder: the rungs above and below goal pace that make goal pace feel sustainable.

Funnel periodization

Funnel periodization, popularised by the Italian coach Renato Canova, approaches a goal race from two sides at once: the aerobic and endurance side, and the anaerobic and speed side. Training starts general and gets more specific as the paces converge on the goal race pace.

One practical write-up describes it as moving from paces far above and below the target toward paces closer and closer to it, until the last phase is fully race-specific. Earlier work is not dropped. A little of each earlier pace is added back every couple of weeks, because keeping a fitness is easier than rebuilding it.

The half marathon and marathon work differently, because there are not several longer race distances to build above. The write-up above puts the emphasis mainly on threshold work for them.

Why the easy-only base is a problem

Runners who only ever run easy meet a shock when quality arrives. Athlevo's Control zone exists to prevent that: it keeps you regularly exposed to moderate intensity, so Threshold does not feel like a cliff.

What Athlevo takes from it

  • Build the whole pace spectrum. Athlevo programs faster-than-target work (Edge) and slower-than-target purposeful work (Control) alongside Threshold through a block.
  • Race pace is a destination. Do not run goal-pace intervals until the rungs above and below are stable.
  • Training adds, it does not replace. New qualities are layered on, not swapped in.
  • Embedded quality in long runs, carefully. For advanced runners in Phase 3, controlled work in the last part of a long run, when you are already tired, is the most race-specific work available. It is used conservatively. Long runs are not turned into workouts by default.
