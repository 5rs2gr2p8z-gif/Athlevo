Running training zones: Ground, Control, Threshold and Edge

By Dean Castro, Founder and Head Coach at Athlevo. Updated 14 November 2026. 3 minute read.

Athlevo uses four training zones: Ground, Control, Threshold and Edge. They are named for what they do, because function is what a runner and a coach need on the road.

In short

  • Ground is easy running. Most of your running lives here.
  • Control is steady work just above easy. It builds fatigue resistance.
  • Threshold is hard but controlled. It is used in small, planned doses.
  • Edge is short, fast and relaxed. It sharpens you. It does not add volume.

Treat the heart rate ranges as a starting reference, not a verdict. How a coach sets your own zones is covered in what a running coach assesses first.

Ground: genuinely easy

Ground running is the base of your week. It builds the aerobic system, supports recovery and, for runners in a hot climate, builds heat tolerance. The test is simple: you can hold a full conversation and nothing feels like pressing.

The most common mistake is letting the top of Ground drift into the bottom of Control on easy days. That creates a grey zone that gives you neither the adaptation of harder work nor the recovery of easy running. If an easy run does not feel easy, the pace is too fast.

The Ground sessions in the Athlevo workout library are the Foundation Run, easy continuous running of 40 to 75 minutes, and the Foundation Long, extended easy running of 75 to 150 minutes or more depending on your level. On a long run the pace should feel easier at 70 minutes than at 20. If it gets harder as it goes, the start was too fast.

When it is above 30°C, pace becomes secondary and effort and breathing set the zone. See running in the heat.

Control: not a junk-mile zone

A Control session has a set duration and a purpose. It is not an easy run that got a bit fast. Two examples from the Athlevo workout library:

  • Stability Cruise: 30 minutes continuous at Control after a Ground warm-up.
  • Stability Blocks: 5 × 8 minutes at Control with 2 minutes of Ground between efforts.

The interval version allows more time in the zone while managing fatigue within the session. It also suits hot weather, because the easy minutes let your body cool between efforts.

Threshold: rationed

Threshold sits near your lactate threshold, where effort is hard but controlled. It is dosed. Athlevo allows at most one or two Threshold sessions a week, never on consecutive days and never when you are depleted. A typical session is 5 × 5 minutes at Threshold with 90 seconds of easy jogging between efforts. More in threshold training.

Edge: the sharpener

Edge work is short, fast and relaxed, above your threshold pace. It develops running economy and top speed, and its volume is always low. A typical session is 8 × 20 seconds at Edge effort with 90 seconds of easy walking or jogging between, or short hill sprints. More in hill sprints and the speed ceiling.

The same zones in lab terms

Coaches and researchers often describe intensity by blood lactate. Athlevo's zones line up with it like this.

Athlevo zones and approximate blood lactate
ZoneWhere it sitsApproximate blood lactate
GroundBelow the first lactate thresholdBelow 2 mmol/L
ControlAround the first lactate thresholdAbout 2 to 2.5 mmol/L
ThresholdNear the second lactate thresholdAbout 3.5 to 5 mmol/L
EdgeAbove the second lactate thresholdShort efforts, set by feel

Most runners do not have a lactate meter, so zones are set from effort, pace and heart rate.
