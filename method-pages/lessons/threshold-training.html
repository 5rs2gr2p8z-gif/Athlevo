Threshold training for runners: the Norwegian model and Athlevo

By Dean Castro, Founder and Head Coach at Athlevo. Updated 14 November 2026. 3 minute read.

Threshold training means running near your lactate threshold, hard but controlled. Athlevo takes one idea from the Norwegian approach: build up a lot of quality work near threshold over time, instead of chasing one maximal session.

In short

  • Threshold work sits near the pace you can hold at your lactate threshold, in controlled intervals.
  • The Norwegian model pairs a lot of easy running with controlled work just below threshold.
  • Athlevo keeps the principle and leaves out the elite-only practices, such as double threshold days and lactate meters.
  • Threshold is introduced after the aerobic base is in place, and it is always rationed.

What threshold running is

Threshold running is work near your lactate threshold, the point where your body starts to make lactate faster than it clears it. It feels hard but controlled, and demands attention. You can hold it for minutes, not hours. In Athlevo it is Zone 3. See the four zones.

The Norwegian idea

The Norwegian threshold model is credited to the Norwegian runner Marius Bakken and is linked with the training of the Ingebrigtsen brothers.

Athlevo reads the model's central argument this way: the best way to raise your threshold is not to train above it, which is costly, slow to recover from and hard to repeat. Instead you build up a high volume of quality work just below it, where the stimulus is there but the stress is low enough to repeat often.

Bakken's principles, as summarised by one running site, keep blood lactate at around 3 mmol/L or below, use intervals rather than continuous running, and split threshold work into shorter sessions so total volume can be higher. Most of the running stays easy. Stephen Seiler's 2010 review of studies of nationally and internationally competitive endurance athletes found training converged on roughly 80% of sessions at low intensity and roughly 20% dominated by high-intensity work.

What Athlevo keeps

  • A weekly Threshold anchor. Once the base is in place, a controlled Threshold session is the anchor of a structured block.
  • Easy days that are truly easy. The Norwegian approach works only because the easy days are easy. Athlevo enforces this through the Ground zone.
  • Clearing lactate when you are tired as a training target. Athlevo does not use double threshold days for most runners. It programs Control work after accumulated load to train this adaptation at a lower dose.
  • Accumulation, not maximization. The goal is a growing volume of work near threshold over time, not the hardest possible single session.

What Athlevo leaves out

The Norwegian model was built for athletes with elite recovery support: controlled environments, twice-daily training, lactate meters for precision and schedules built around training. Athlevo runners are working adults, running at 6am in the heat before a 10-hour workday. They do not have that setup. Athlevo takes the principle and leaves the elite-specific practices.

Threshold sessions in the Athlevo library

  • Pressure Waves: classic threshold intervals. Example: 5 × 5 minutes at Threshold with 90 seconds of easy jogging between. The recoveries are jogged, not walked.
  • Pressure Ladder: a descending sequence that spreads fatigue across the session. Example: 6, 5, 4, 3 and 2 minutes at Threshold with 90 seconds of jogging between. Useful for runners who start flat interval sessions too fast.
  • Pressure Cruise: continuous Threshold running with no recovery break. Example: 20 to 25 minutes. Only for advanced runners with high available capacity.

When Threshold comes in

Athlevo builds aerobic durability and fatigue resistance first. Threshold work comes after both are stable, and it is always rationed. In Athlevo's view, bringing it in too early is a common coaching error: runners feel fit for a short time before they break down.
